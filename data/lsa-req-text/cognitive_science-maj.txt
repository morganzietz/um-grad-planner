Requirements Minimum Credits: 27 The major consists of 9 courses (a minimum of 27 credits), structured into four tracks. Each track (Computation, Decision, Philosophy, Language) represents a major area of research within contemporary cognitive science.Each track consists of:3 Required Track Courses6 Electives4 elective courses chosen from a track-specific list.2 elective courses chosen from the Cognitive Science MasterlistNote: The combined set of students' required and elective courses must be selected from a minimum of three departments (example: students on the Decision and Cognition track must complete at least one course from outside of PSYCH and PHIL).Computation and Cognition TrackA foundational idea of cognitive science is that mental processes are computational, and computation remains central to (but not the exclusive domain of) the field. The Computation and Cognition track requires students to take coursework in psychology and computer programming. Subsequent depth courses emphasize — although not exclusively so — computational and formal methods including machine learning, computational linguistics, rational choice theory, and artificial intelligence.Required Track Courses (3 courses)Choose One:PSYCH 240: Introduction to Cognitive PsychologyPSYCH 345: Introduction to Human NeuropsychologyEECS 281: Data Structures and AlgorithmsChoose One:EECS 492: Introduction to Artificial IntelligenceEECS 445: Introduction to Machine LearningCOGSCI 445: Introduction to Machine Learning for Natural Language ProcessingElectives (6 courses)Choose 4 elective courses chosen from the Computation Track listChoose 2 elective courses chosen from the Cog Sci MasterlistDecision and Cognition TrackThe study of decision and choice is a lively area of contemporary cognitive science inquiry, and the Decision and Cognition track provides students with sustained engagement with this field. Students are presented with theoretical approaches to judgment and decision-making from psychology, neurocircuit models of reward and reinforcement from neurobiology, algorithmic models of planning and action selection from computer science, formal approaches to rational choice from philosophy, political science, and cutting-edge approaches to understanding irrationality from behavioral economics. The required courses in the Decision and Cognition track give students an introduction to historically influential approaches to decision-making drawn from psychology and philosophy. Students then have the opportunity to take coursework in a number of disciplines that approach decision-making from diverse but complementary theoretical and applied perspectives.Required Track Courses (3 courses)Choose One:PHIL 215: Practicing EthicsPHIL 361: EthicsChoose One:PHIL 342: Introduction to Theory of Choice in GroupsPHIL 343: Introduction to Rational Choice TheoryPHIL 443: Foundations of Rational Choice TheoryPHIL 444: Groups and ChoicesPSYCH 449: Decision ProcessesElectives (6 courses)Choose 4 elective courses chosen from the Decision Track listChoose 2 elective courses chosen from the Cog Sci MasterlistLanguage and Cognition TrackBecause human language is universal in the species and grounded in human cognition and biology, linguistic inquiry was an integral component of the cognitive science revolution. Contemporary approaches to language synthesize models and findings from multiple disciplines, and the proposed curriculum is correspondingly interdisciplinary. The Language and Cognition track gives students a solid theoretical introduction to language through required coursework in linguistics, and in the philosophy and psychology of language. Further coursework broadens the investigation of language to include topics in computational linguistics and computer science, formal methods, and language development and learning.Required Track Courses (3 Courses)Choose one:LING 313: Sound PatternsLING 315: Introduction to SyntaxLING 316: Aspects of MeaningChoose one:PHIL 345: Language and MindPHIL 409: Philosophy of LanguagePHIL 426 / LING 426: Philosophy & Linguistic TheoryLING 209 / PSYCH 242 / COGSCI 209: Language and the Human MindChoose one:LING 347 / PSYCH 349: Talking MindsLING / PSYCH 352: Development of Language and ThoughtLING / COGSCI / NEURO 432 / PSYCH 444: Introduction to NeurolinguisticsElectives (6 courses) Choose 4 elective courses from the Language Track list Choose 2 elective courses chosen from the Cog Sci MasterlistPhilosophy and Cognition TrackThere is extensive interaction between contemporary philosophy, especially philosophy of mind and ethics, and cognitive science. Philosophers have long posed fundamental questions about the nature of minds, consciousness, the relationship between the mental and physical, and the nature of human agency. Cognitive science provides a rich and ever-expanding body of theory, models, and findings that are relevant to these timeless philosophical questions. The Philosophy and Cognition track requires coursework in core philosophical, formal and cognitive approaches to mind. More in-depth coursework allows students to deepen their understanding of the philosophical problems and analytical enigmas raised by language and other symbolic systems, artificial intelligence, inference and reasoning, and decision-making.Required Track Courses (3 courses)Choose One:PSYCH 240: Introduction to Cognitive PsychologyPSYCH 345: Introduction to Human NeuropsychologyChoose One:PHIL 340: Minds and MachinesPHIL 482: Philosophy of MindChoose One:PHIL 303: Introduction to Symbolic LogicPHIL 305: Introduction to Formal Philosophical MethodsPHIL 322: The Methods of SciencePHIL 413: Formal Philosophical MethodsElectives (6 courses) Choose 4 elective courses chosen from the Philosophy Track list Choose 2 elective courses chosen from the Cog Sci Masterlist Constraints Double-Majoring.
